Dead startups almost never drop their .com

People point at names like gpt.com or droid.com and assume someone forgot to pay. I checked the official registry (RDAP) on 16 August 2026.

  • gpt.com — registered 1999, expires 2028, MarkMonitor, Cloudflare. The site sends you to ChatGPT. Not leftover.
  • droid.com — registered 1998, expires 2027, CSC corporate lock, server-delete prohibited. Sends you to Star Wars. Not leftover.
  • siri.com — Apple nameservers. Expires December 2026. They will renew it.
  • vine.com — Amazon DNS. The app is dead. The name is not.
  • homejoy.com — the cleaning startup died years ago. The domain is paid through 2035.
  • subtl.ai — press said the domain expired after the 2025 shutdown. Today it sits on Afternic until 2029. That is a sales lot, not a free grab.
  • omedym.com — shutdown 2025, now on NameBright. Still taken through 2027.

When a company dies, the name usually goes to: the acquirer, a lawyer, a domain investor, or a parking page. The last step before “you can register it at list price” is an auction. That is not $0 and it is not quiet.

What I will not hunt

Names that sound like living retailers. That is cybersquatting. It gets the domain taken from you and can get you sued. This site will look up a real name you type. It will not generate a list of Target/Walmart/Amazon lookalikes.

What you can actually do

  • Use the graveyard lookup on a specific dead company you already know about.
  • Use the cheap-TLD hunter if you want a name that is truly unregistered.
  • Use eu.org if you need $0 forever.

The broader version of this is there is no leftover $0 .com pile. The names that are still unregistered live on cheap TLDs — that is how the hunter works.

Sources: Verisign / PIR / Identity Digital RDAP on 2026-08-16; live HTTP for gpt.com and droid.com.
